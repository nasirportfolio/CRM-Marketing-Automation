Businesses rarely have customers who are exactly the same. Customers can differ in their needs, interests, location, purchase history, engagement, and relationship with a business.

Customer segmentation provides a way to organize customers into groups that share relevant characteristics or behaviors. This can help businesses understand different groups and create more appropriate communication and customer experiences.

What Is Customer Segmentation?

Customer segmentation is the process of dividing a customer base into groups of customers who share similar characteristics, behaviors, needs, or other relevant attributes.

Instead of treating every customer as one large group, a business can create smaller segments and use those segments for marketing, sales, service, or customer relationship activities.

Simple definition: Customer segmentation means grouping customers with similar characteristics or behaviors so a business can better understand and serve each group.

Why Is Customer Segmentation Important?

A single message or offer may not be equally relevant to every customer. Segmentation can help businesses make their communication more relevant to particular groups.

For example, a business may want to communicate differently with a new customer, a long-term customer, and a customer who has not purchased recently.

Segmentation can also help teams analyze customer groups and understand differences in their behavior, needs, and responses. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

How Does Customer Segmentation Work?

A basic segmentation process can follow several steps.

  1. Define the business goal for segmentation.
  2. Identify the customer data that is relevant to that goal.
  3. Select meaningful characteristics or behaviors.
  4. Create groups based on defined rules.
  5. Develop appropriate actions for each segment.
  6. Measure results and review the segments regularly.

The important point is that segmentation should have a practical purpose. A segment is more useful when it helps a business make a decision about communication, service, sales, or another customer-related activity.

Types of Customer Segmentation

Businesses can segment customers in several ways. The appropriate method depends on the type of business, available data, and the purpose of the segmentation.

What Is Demographic Segmentation?

Demographic segmentation groups customers according to relevant demographic characteristics.

Depending on the business and the data it legitimately collects, examples can include age range, income range, education level, job role, or company characteristics in a B2B context.

Businesses should use only information that is appropriate, relevant, and collected in accordance with applicable privacy requirements.

What Is Geographic Segmentation?

Geographic segmentation groups customers according to location, such as country, region, state, city, or service area.

This can be useful when products, services, availability, language, delivery options, or campaigns differ by location.

For example, a business operating in several regions might create separate customer groups for each service area.

What Is Behavioral Segmentation?

Behavioral segmentation groups customers according to actions or interactions.

Examples can include purchase frequency, website activity, content engagement, product usage, email interaction, or recency of purchase.

Behavioral segmentation can be particularly useful when customer actions provide a strong signal about current interests or engagement.

What Is Psychographic Segmentation?

Psychographic segmentation considers characteristics such as interests, values, preferences, lifestyles, or attitudes.

This type of segmentation can help businesses understand differences that may not be visible from basic demographic information alone.

Because psychographic information can be more difficult to collect accurately, businesses should use appropriate sources and avoid making unsupported assumptions about customers.

What Is Needs-Based Segmentation?

Needs-based segmentation groups customers according to the problems they are trying to solve or the outcomes they want.

For example, a software company might have one group looking for basic features and another group looking for advanced automation capabilities.

This approach can help businesses focus communication on the needs that are most relevant to each group.

What Is Transactional Segmentation?

Transactional segmentation uses information from customer purchases and transactions.

Common measures can include how recently a customer purchased, how frequently they purchase, and how much they have spent. These concepts are often referred to as recency, frequency, and monetary value, or RFM. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

For example, a business could distinguish between recent active customers, occasional customers, and customers who have not purchased for a long period.

Customer Segmentation in CRM

CRM software can provide the customer information needed to create useful segments.

Depending on the CRM platform, businesses may use fields, tags, customer attributes, purchase information, interaction history, or filters to organize customers into groups.

A CRM can therefore turn customer information into groups that sales, marketing, and customer-service teams can use for specific activities.

Customer Segmentation and Marketing Automation

Marketing automation can use customer segments to deliver different communications to different groups.

For example, a business could create one automated workflow for new customers and another for customers who have not purchased recently.

Automation can also update segment membership when customer information or behavior changes, depending on the capabilities of the platform.

Customer Segmentation Examples

Example 1: New Customers

A business can create a segment for customers who recently made their first purchase. This group could receive onboarding information or product education.

Example 2: Frequent Customers

A business can identify customers who purchase regularly and create a segment for loyalty or retention activities.

Example 3: Inactive Customers

Customers who have not purchased or interacted with a business for a defined period can be grouped for a re-engagement strategy.

Example 4: Product Interest

Customers can be grouped according to products or categories they have purchased, viewed, or shown interest in.

Example 5: Geographic Area

A company operating across multiple locations can create segments based on service regions and adapt communications where appropriate.

Common Customer Segmentation Mistakes

Creating Segments Without a Purpose

Creating many groups without knowing how they will be used can make a CRM difficult to manage. Each segment should have a clear business purpose.

Using Poor-Quality Data

Incorrect, incomplete, or outdated customer data can lead to inaccurate segments.

Using Too Many Segments

Extremely detailed segmentation can become difficult to maintain. Businesses should balance relevance with simplicity.

Never Updating Segments

Customer behavior changes. A customer who was once inactive may become active again. Segments should therefore be reviewed and, where appropriate, updated dynamically.

Making Unsupported Assumptions

Businesses should rely on relevant data rather than assuming that everyone in a particular group has identical needs or preferences.

Customer Segmentation Best Practices

  1. Start with a clear business or customer-experience goal.
  2. Use relevant and reliable customer data.
  3. Choose segmentation criteria that can lead to useful action.
  4. Keep segment rules simple enough for teams to understand.
  5. Combine multiple relevant criteria when appropriate.
  6. Review segments regularly as customer behavior changes.
  7. Measure how each segment responds to the actions associated with it.
  8. Respect privacy requirements and use customer data responsibly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is customer segmentation in simple terms?

Customer segmentation means dividing customers into groups that share relevant characteristics, behaviors, or needs.

Why do businesses use customer segmentation?

Businesses use segmentation to better understand different customer groups and make marketing, sales, service, or communication activities more relevant.

What are the main types of customer segmentation?

Common approaches include demographic, geographic, behavioral, psychographic, needs-based, and transactional segmentation.

Can CRM software be used for customer segmentation?

Yes. CRM systems can store customer information and, depending on the platform, provide fields, filters, tags, or other features that can be used to organize customers into groups.

Can customer segmentation be automated?

Yes. Some CRM and marketing automation systems can update customer groups automatically when customer information or behavior meets predefined rules.

What is RFM segmentation?

RFM segmentation groups customers using recency, frequency, and monetary value of their transactions. It is one approach to analyzing customer purchasing behavior.

Conclusion

Customer segmentation helps businesses organize customers into meaningful groups based on shared characteristics, behaviors, needs, or transaction patterns.

Segmentation can support more relevant communication and help teams understand differences between customer groups.

CRM software can provide the customer information needed to create and manage segments, while marketing automation can use those segments to support targeted workflows and communication.

The most useful segmentation strategy is not necessarily the most complicated one. It should be based on reliable data, have a clear purpose, and be reviewed as customer behavior changes.
